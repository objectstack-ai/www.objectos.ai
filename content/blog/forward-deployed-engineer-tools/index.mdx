**The short version:** the forward-deployed engineer is the fastest-growing role in enterprise AI — reported hiring growth of roughly 1,000% year over year, and OpenAI committed $4B to a dedicated deployment company in May 2026. The playbook that actually works is Palantir's: **ontology-first** — model the client's business before shipping any AI application. What the playbook leaves unsolved is the ending. Every engagement finishes with a handover, and today the ontology usually stays locked inside a platform the client rents forever. We think the defining question for the next wave of FDEs is what we call **the ontology handover**: does the client's ontology leave the engagement as typed, open files in *their* repository — or as renewal leverage in someone else's?

## The role that ate enterprise AI

The numbers, as reported this year: FDE job postings grew more than 800% between January and September 2025 and passed 1,000% year over year in early 2026 ([Perspective AI](https://getperspective.ai/blog/2026-fde-hiring-trends-what-1000-job-posts-reveal), [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchenterpriseai/feature/The-rise-of-the-AI-forward-deployed-engineer)). In May 2026 OpenAI announced a deployment company with **$4B in committed capital** and ~150 FDEs. The reason is structural: models got good enough that the bottleneck moved from modeling to deployment — every enterprise has unique data, workflows, and compliance constraints, and someone has to sit inside them and make AI fit.

## The playbook that works is ontology-first

Palantir codified it years before the term was fashionable: their AI FDEs **front-load a customer-specific ontology before shipping any LLM-grounded application**, and spend 30–40% of the week on discovery ([Palantir's AI FDE guidance](https://www.palantir.com/docs/foundry/ai-fde/overview)). The insight is durable: generic models give generic answers. Enterprise value requires grounding in the customer's nouns and verbs — objects, relationships, permissions, approval paths. That is engineering, not prompting.

None of that is controversial anymore. What is rarely discussed is what happens at the end.

## The unsolved part: the handover

Every forward-deployed engagement ends. The FDE rolls off, and the ontology they built — the most valuable artifact of the whole engagement, the encoded knowledge of how this business actually runs — lands in one of two places:

- **Inside the vendor's platform.** The client can use it, but only through the platform, priced per seat, changed only by platform-trained specialists. The ontology has become renewal leverage.
- **In the client's repository.** Typed, versioned, open files the client's own team — and the client's own AI agents — can read, run, and keep changing without anyone's permission.

The first outcome is the default today, and it is why enterprise clients have learned to fear successful pilots: the better the ontology, the deeper the lock-in. The second outcome is only possible if the definition layer itself is an open format.

## What an open FDE stack looks like

This is the workflow [ObjectStack](https://github.com/objectstack-ai/objectstack) (Apache-2.0) is built for. Discovery notes become typed metadata — your coding agent writes it, a validation gate rejects what would fail silently:

```ts
export const Ticket = ObjectSchema.create({
  name: 'support_ticket',
  label: 'Ticket',
  sharingModel: 'private',        // conservative by default — the gate requires it
  fields: {
    subject:  Field.text({ label: 'Subject', required: true }),
    status:   Field.select({ label: 'Status', options: [/* the client's real states */] }),
    approver: Field.lookup('sys_user'),
  },
});
```

From that definition the runtime derives the database, REST APIs, admin Console, and MCP tools, and enforces permissions and audit on every call — including after you leave. The whole app stays reviewable: a complete CRM is **1,792 lines, roughly 16k tokens** — count it yourself:

```bash
find examples/app-crm/src -name '*.ts' -not -name '*.test.ts' | xargs cat | wc -l
```

That size is what makes the handover honest. The client's security team reads the actual diff instead of trusting a platform boundary; the client's future agents load the entire system into context and refactor it whole.

## What this does not solve

Being clear about the boundary: **massive-scale data federation and analytics pipelines** are Foundry's home turf, and an open application layer does not replace them. **Organizational change management** — getting people to use the thing — remains human work no stack fixes. And a client already standardized on a closed platform may rationally stay. The claim is narrower: for the application layer of forward-deployed work — modeling a business and shipping governed apps on it — the ontology can now be handed over instead of held hostage.

## The FDE checklist

1. Model the client's nouns and verbs as objects and flows before any UI conversation.
2. Keep the whole definition context-sized so your agent can refactor it whole.
3. Default permissions conservative; make every authority change explicit in the diff.
4. Hand over the repo, the compiled artifact, and a review checklist — not a login to your tenant.
5. Leave MCP enabled so the client's own AI operates the app under their permissions.
